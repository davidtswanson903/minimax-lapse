\begin{enumerate}
  \item\label{premise:evolution} The whole, the system with its probes and the clock a description reads, is stationary under the saddle's weighted generator plus the clock's own energy, with the system at its floor; time is that clock's reading, and the rest conditioned on it evolves as $e^{-iA_{N^*}t}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:conditioned}).
  \item\label{premise:probes} A clock or a thermometer at a place is an idle test qubit of frequency $\omega$, whose energy counts in that place's density and which couples to nothing else; such probes leave the weights unchanged (Appendix~\ref{app:probes}).
  \item\label{premise:equilibrium} In equilibrium the system and its probes are in the thermal state of the weighted generator at a global inverse temperature $\beta$, and a thermometer reads the temperature at which its own populations are thermal with respect to its own energy.
  \item\label{premise:constant} The part of the common value set by the whole's size, rather than by any one place's terms, is the negative of a cosmological constant, the energy conjugate to the clock's reading; its value in the units of $\Lambda$ would need Newton's constant, which the paper does not bring in.
  \item\label{premise:subsystem} A measured entanglement Hamiltonian is the maximum-entropy state of its part's measured local terms, so its weights are multipliers on local energies, of the same kind as the saddle's.
  \item\label{premise:cone} At every place the long waves move at the speed of light, read by the clock at that place; where the weights are uniform, the whole has one light cone at the scales concerned, the speed $v$ of its long waves.
  \item\label{premise:patch} A part's horizon, the edge at which its weights vanish, is the cosmological horizon, whose surface gravity general relativity sets at $c\sqrt{\Lambda/3}$.
  \item\label{premise:records} A reading is a record, the same for every description, exactly where a full copy of it, one whose states tell its outcomes apart, exists outside its own place.
\end{enumerate}
